# Personas, Voice & Brand (deep reference)

Read this when a post needs to hit a precise fear or ambition, when you want to deepen the voice, or
when you need the full storytelling framework. The compact versions live in SKILL.md.

## Persona One: Ex-Corporate John

- **Age / stage:** ~48, married with teenage kids. Very senior, ex-CMO or Marketing Director, now an
  agency owner or independent consultant building a post-corporate venture.
- **Primary goal:** Build a legacy he's proud of, the thing that made leaving corporate worth it. Do
  impactful work. More time for family.
- **Motivations:** Freedom from corporate handcuffs, more family time, meaningful work.
- **Struggles:** Adapting to digital tools he never had to touch in corporate (his agencies did SEO,
  Google Ads, websites for him). Selling his own services. Inconsistent systems. Business growth.
- **Strengths:** Strategic, experienced, excellent communicator and storyteller. Curious, wants to learn.
- **Weaknesses:** Struggles to delegate, over-relies on others for execution. Can underestimate what
  it takes to scale a modern digital business. Risks becoming a "traffic cop" who manages without
  empowering a real team.
- **Values:** Family, work ethic, legacy.
- **Preferred content:** Thought-leadership insights, videos.
- **Objections:** Wary of flashy consultants. Prefers grounded experts.

Write for John when the topic is: strategy, legacy, delegation, modernizing without losing his edge,
the gap between knowing marketing and executing it, building something meaningful after corporate.

## Persona Two: Startup Corey

- **Age / stage:** ~27-35, some married, some with young kids. Mid-level, transitioning from employee
  to entrepreneur. Founder/co-founder/freelancer building a digital agency or SaaS startup.
- **Primary goal:** Build a profitable business, escape the feast/famine cycle, escape working for a
  boss and fitting into corporate structures.
- **Motivations:** Financial freedom, flexibility, personal brand growth. Living the entrepreneurial dream.
- **Struggles:** Pricing strategy, thin margins when outsourcing, managing pipeline, doing everything
  alone, inconsistent delivery (too many freelancers, too many hats). Unstable feast-or-famine income.
- **Strengths:** Technically skilled (design, dev, marketing). Highly motivated, eager to learn,
  values practical advice.
- **Weaknesses:** Imposter syndrome, burnout, no structured business systems. Struggles to price and
  sell his value.
- **Values:** Transparency, speed, freedom, learning from peers.
- **Preferred content:** Behind-the-scenes, how-tos, templates, case studies.
- **Objections:** Skeptical of upsells, worried about ROI, burned by past bad experiences.

Write for Corey when the topic is: pricing, recurring revenue, systems, delegation, burnout, imposter
syndrome, the employee-to-owner leap, getting off the feast/famine rollercoaster.

## What they share (the overlap)
Impact-driven, goal-oriented, growth mindset, strategy-focused, practical-focused, action takers.
Both want clear, results-driven help over vague theory. Both are silent "lurkers" who need an easy,
low-risk reason to engage.

## How they want to see Leon
A trusted expert who simplifies complexity and gives practical, no-nonsense advice. Someone who
genuinely understands their world. A relatable, values-led partner, not a flashy consultant or
know-it-all. A strategic guide and relentlessly helpful growth mentor who helps them beat overwhelm
and make smarter decisions. Honest, supportive, empowering, bridging vision and execution.

## The storytelling framework (full)

### Start lines
Lead with I, You, or We where possible.

### Hooks (earn the "see more" click)
A post with no hook is a hook with no fish. Even a great post dies if the hook is weak. To write one:
- Start in the middle of the action.
- Create a "gather round" feeling.
- Open an information gap (tease something the reader needs the rest of the post to close).
- Lead with the punchline.

Aim every hook at one of four things:
- Their biggest **fear** (to alleviate it)
- Their biggest **dream** (to mirror it)
- Their biggest **belief** (to champion it)
- Their biggest **knowledge gap** (to teach them)

### The 6 elements of an engaging story
1. **A relatable character.** Usually Leon or a client. Must be a real person, never a product or
   abstract idea. The audience should see themselves in them. Leon is the guide, not the hero, the
   audience is the hero.
2. **Specific details.** Feelings, objects, numbers, frustrations, routines. They prove "this person
   gets me."
3. **Goldilocks emotion.** Genuine and authentic. Not over-exaggerated, not flat.
4. **The complication.** The problem to overcome, with real stakes. The moment of highest tension.
5. **A brief moment of significant change.** The specific few-second moment Leon realized what he had
   to do. Locate it precisely.
6. **Lesson learned.** The realization, tied to a business point of value the community can use.

### The structure
**Normal → Complication → New Normal.**
- **Normal:** the world before. Setting and relatable character established. Tension rising.
- **Complication:** the problem the character faces, what's at risk, tension peaks.
- **New Normal:** the world after, usually the mirror image of Normal, ending on the lesson/business point.
  (E.g. "Before, I spent 4 hours a day driving between businesses. New normal: I work from home and
  use that time productively. Here's what that taught me.")

## Anti-Leon (the full edit-against list)

Personality & presence:
- Not arrogant or cocky. Backs himself, but isn't the loudest voice in the room.
- Not "Mr. Know-It-All." He loves facts and jumps to solutions, but never makes anyone feel small.
- Cares deeply about people. Coming across like he doesn't give a damn would feel completely wrong.

Content:
- Doesn't preach "the one right way." There's rarely one way to do anything in marketing.
- Doesn't sell hustle culture. Works hard, sometimes weekends, but shows up for family. Respects balance.
- Not the unsolicited-advice guy ("if you're not doing 10k steps a day, you're doing life wrong").

With clients / presence:
- Not flashy. Doesn't flaunt cars, watches, or power moves.
- Not the formal suit-and-tie type. Business-smart when needed, full corporate costume never.
- Not fake-polished. Relatable, real, self-aware.

When editing any draft, ask: would Anti-Leon have written this line? If yes, cut or rewrite it.
