# Leon's Story Bank ("Cupcakes")

These are Leon's real stories from his Story Finder Tool and Ideas Bank. They're the cupcakes he's
happy to share. Reach for a real one before inventing a generic angle, it's what makes a post
uniquely Leon and earns trust with John and Corey.

Use them honestly. Don't embellish facts, add fake numbers, or change how something actually felt.
If a post needs a detail that isn't here, ask Leon rather than making it up.

Each story can map to a track and a structure (usually Normal → Complication → New Normal). Notes
on the angle are in parentheses.

---

## Origin & the climb out

- **Tech blog in high school (2011-2012).** Started a tech blog as a teenager. It got him press
  credentials and into CES, IFA, and SXSW, talking and making content alongside people from Engadget
  and other tech blogs. First "I made it" moment was that first CES. The blog led to a job in agency
  marketing in 2018. (Co-Founder / Expert. The unlikely origin story.)
- **Dropped out of college.** Didn't know what he wanted, but knew he didn't want to just be an
  employee. Wanted his own thing and control over his work. (Co-Founder. Anti-conventional path.)
- **Working nights at HEB.** ~5 years of tech blogging while working night shifts at HEB to make
  ends meet. Slept during the day, wrote when he could. Hated the work but it paid. On those nights
  he'd listen to podcasts and try to better himself, watching others trying to escape the same rat
  race. (Co-Founder / Lead Gen. Deeply relatable to Corey.)
- **First "I made it" moment.** First CES with press credentials, standing alongside the tech
  bloggers he'd admired. (Co-Founder.)
- **Finding his people online.** Working with Earth Class Mail (which was selling to LegalZoom), the
  developers and founders he admired on Twitter actually talked to him. Got a client from Product
  Hunt and realized those builders cared about the same things he did, desk setups, productivity. He
  felt seen, like he fit in. (Co-Founder / Expert. Belonging and community.)

## Becoming an owner

- **Becoming a dad at 21.** A huge thing. It motivated him to push harder for success and made him a
  better leader, taught him patience and how to handle different people's styles. When the first kid
  came he was 21, working nights, sleeping days, writing to get through. Family supported him but he
  always wanted more. (Co-Founder. His most human, relatable story.)
- **From employee to owner.** Working at the agency, he realized he could get clients himself. Went
  out on his own and discovered he "owned a job more than anything," so he started making real moves
  to build a business. (Expert / Co-Founder.)
- **The mental load of owning.** "Going from working to owning is a huge shock. A way bigger mental
  load. Even a corporate job lets you disconnect. Owning makes it stressful in your personal life,
  cash flow, vendors, clients. There is no off." (Expert. The honest cost.)
- **COVID as a turning point.** Moved, bought a house, and went all-in building the business. Remote
  life changed everything. They came out strong by hunkering down and working. (Co-Founder.)

## Buying, building, and a deal that went wrong

- **Buying into the marketing agency.** The biggest decision. The owner had a base of customers but
  was only generating ~$10k. A gamble on whether the processes existed, but he had hiring down. It
  gave them both a base to scale from. (Expert. Calculated risk.)
- **Buying the accounting/tax firm.** Kept running into businesses with messy books that didn't know
  if they were actually growing because nothing was tracked properly. It tied directly into the
  marketing problem. (Expert. Why tracking matters.)
- **The agency acquisition that failed.** They tried to acquire an agency and lost in the end because
  the owner was the linchpin to everything. Once he got the money he started backing out of work and
  left clients hanging. Lesson: vet the processes and systems, and background-check people before
  handing over that kind of money. (Expert. "3 mistakes when acquiring an agency.")
- **Learning there's no formula.** Working with his first private equity firm was life changing,
  seeing the systems they used to turn around struggling SaaS and small businesses. The realization:
  most businesses are just chaos, everyone doing what they think is best, no one has a formula.
  (Expert / Lead Gen. The big reframe.)
- **The big-contract myth.** "I've had the huge contracts. They don't solve everything, they create
  more headaches. The sales take long and due diligence is terrible. But building a thoughtful
  lifestyle business is possible." (Expert. Anti-hype.)

## His dad & work ethic

- **"Trust yourself first."** His dad always told him he couldn't trust anyone but himself, that no
  one gives handouts. Still true in most businesses: build things first, then hire people to run them.
  (Expert.)
- **"Never trust one thing."** His dad never took any nonsense and would line up a new job within
  weeks of leaving one. He said never to rely on one thing, being an employee is high-risk because
  it's a single revenue stream tied to one employer. (Expert. Risk management.)
- **Dad was always there.** Best memory is playing sports with his dad always involved. Owning a
  business means Leon gets to be there for his kids too, living a lifestyle business, while knowing
  that's not what everyone is building. (Co-Founder. Family + the why.)
- **First job, cutting grass at 15.** Middle-class childhood. Started cutting lawns at 15 to earn
  money, used it (with his dad covering half) to buy a car. Learned that just getting out and asking
  people for the work was all it took. Got his work ethic from his father. (Co-Founder.)

## Honest about himself

- **Money lessons came late.** Money conversations never happened growing up, so when he first started
  earning he just spent rather than saved. That flipped when a mentor helped him invest and make his
  money work for him. (Expert / Co-Founder. Vulnerability + lesson.)
- **The perfectionism flip.** Had a perfectionism streak that flipped too far the other way, sometimes
  not checking work, trusting too much, and getting burned. "Inspect what you expect." (Expert.)
- **Jumps to solutions.** Tends to jump straight to solving and sometimes needs to sit in the problem
  space longer. (Co-Founder. Self-aware, not self-flagellating.)
- **Thrives on stress, but.** "It's stressful, but I thrive on it. Many burn out and things change,
  so stay healthy and let loose." (Co-Founder. Balance, not hustle.)

## Stories his audience always finds fascinating

- **Employee issues / wild behavior.** The employee who downloaded all client data and tried to take
  it to another agency. Shocking and brazen. People are always fascinated. (Expert. Great hook,
  lesson about processes and trust.)
- **Inside private equity.** People want to understand where VCs and PEs are heading, it gives Leon
  talking points about what investors are thinking. (Expert. Insider knowledge.)
- **Real ad-spend numbers.** Explaining that ad spend can easily be $100k+ even for a mid-sized
  company. Behind-the-scenes / inside-baseball always works. (Expert / Lead Gen.)

## How to use a cupcake in a post

1. Pick a story that matches the persona's struggle or dream.
2. Open on the hook (often the most surprising or specific line).
3. Tell it Normal → Complication → New Normal.
4. Land on the lesson or business point for John/Corey.
5. Close with an easy question that invites them to share their own version.
6. Suggest a real photo where one exists (young Leon, family, CES, the team).

Personal-life flags to weave in for relatability (per the Playbook): Leon the family man, the SIM
racer who loves living on a farm, the multiple-pet owner, the multi-business operator with real
battle scars, the guy who gives other entrepreneurs a platform through The Boost.
