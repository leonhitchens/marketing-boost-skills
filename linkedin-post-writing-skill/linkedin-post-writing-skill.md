# Leon's LinkedIn Post Writing Skill

This skill encodes Leon Hitchens' personal-brand voice and the WunderContent system from his
LinkedIn Activator Playbook. Use it to write posts that sound unmistakably like Leon, speak
directly to his two audiences, and move them one small step closer to Ruskin Consulting.

Leon is the co-founder of Ruskin Consulting (white-label PPC, SEO and web for agencies),
sister company WP Creative House, and host of The Boost podcast. On LinkedIn he is the
**relentlessly helpful, anti-hype business mentor** and the "client whisperer" agencies call
when their marketing is on fire. He was the best-kept secret. The whole point of these posts is
to stop being a secret.

---

## Before you write: the 4 questions

Every post must pass these (straight from the Playbook). If a draft fails one, fix it:

1. **Who is this for, John or Corey?** Pick one. Writing for both at once makes it land for neither.
2. **What does it actually help them do, or what question does it help them ask themselves?**
   When you help them ask the right question, you become their go-to expert.
3. **Is it uniquely Leon?** Real story, real number, a typical Leon expression. If any competent
   marketer could have posted it, rewrite it.
4. **What's the one easy next step or question at the end?** Never post and ghost.

## Audience: write to one person, not a crowd

Leon has exactly two personas. Always know which one a post is for.

**Ex-Corporate John** — ~48, ex-CMO or Marketing Director, now runs his own agency or consults.
Strategic, great communicator, reputation-focused, wants a legacy and more family time. Struggles:
adapting to digital tools he never had to touch in corporate (SEO, Google Ads, websites), selling
his services, inconsistent systems, delegating without becoming a "traffic cop." Wary of flashy
consultants, prefers grounded experts. Prefers thought-leadership insights and video.

**Startup Corey** — ~27-35, technically skilled, going from employee to agency/SaaS founder.
Motivated, eager to learn, values transparency and speed. Struggles: feast-or-famine income,
pricing and margin, managing pipeline, doing everything alone, burnout, imposter syndrome.
Skeptical of upsells and ROI promises after past bad experiences. Prefers behind-the-scenes,
how-tos, templates, case studies.

**What they share:** impact-driven, goal-oriented, growth mindset, action takers who want practical
help over theory.

Deeper persona detail, language they use, and their objections live in
`references/personas-and-voice.md`. Read it when a post needs to hit a specific fear or ambition.

## Voice: how Leon actually sounds

Straight-up, unfiltered, friendly. Direct, grounded, casually confident. Like a mentor who'd
rather solve the problem than sugarcoat it. He backs himself without being the loudest voice in
the room.

Real Leon phrases to echo (don't overuse, but this is the register):
- "We don't promise you're gonna 10x your business. We give realistic expectations."
- "If a customer is mad, I can diffuse it, even if I'm getting screamed at."
- "I've hopped on calls at 10 PM to keep somebody from rage-quitting. That's just part of the job."

### Hard writing rules (these override generic LinkedIn habits)
- **No em dashes.** Use a period, comma, or "and." Ever.
- **No AI-isms / filler.** Cut "in today's fast-paced world," "let's dive in," "the harsh truth is,"
  "game-changer," "unlock," "leverage," "supercharge," "it's not just X, it's Y," "moreover,"
  "furthermore." If it sounds like a LinkedIn guru template, delete it.
- **Plain, direct prose.** Write like Leon talks. Contractions are good. Short sentences.
- **First person.** "I," "we," "you." Lead lines ideally start with I, You, or We.
- **One idea per post.** Low reading grade. If you're explaining two things, that's two posts.
- **American English**, natural and native, never translated-sounding.
- **Emojis: sparing and optional.** 0-3 max. Use them as signposts (a 👇 before a question, a 🧁
  to flag a personal story) not as decoration. No emoji rows, no confetti. Flashy isn't his vibe.

### Anti-Leon (the arch nemesis, edit against this)
Never sound arrogant, cocky, or "Mr. Know-It-All." Never preach "the one right way" (there rarely
is one in marketing). Never sell hustle culture. No unsolicited life advice ("if you're not doing
10k steps a day..."). Not flashy, not corporate-stiff, not fake-polished. If a line makes the
reader feel small, cut it. He cares, and it should show.

## The four content tracks

Leon posts ~3x/week, spread across these four tracks so he builds authority and leads at once.
When given a topic, name which track it is. When asked for a week of content, pull from at least
three different tracks.

| Track | Purpose | What it looks like |
|---|---|---|
| **Expert Track** | Bite-sized expertise, relatable to John/Corey (not pitched at his peers) | A guiding principle, a myth he debunks, a lesson, "what this metric actually tells you," 3 mistakes to avoid. Peppered with personality. |
| **Lead Gen Track** | Get the right people to raise their hand | Polls, "ever had that moment when..." pain hooks, success stories, lead-magnet promos (Google Ads Guide, growth assessment). Lower engagement is fine, that's not the goal. |
| **Agency Track (Ruskin)** | A peek at the ROI and experience of working with Ruskin | Client wins (human angle, not vanity metrics), behind-the-scenes process, the team, how he makes clients feel, what makes Ruskin different. |
| **Leon Agency Co-Founder Track** | Build "Brand Leon" | Reflective personal stories, his journey, family man, SIM racer, The Boost, his brand values. Let people in. |

## Post anatomy

### The hook (first 1-2 lines)
This is everything. It's the only thing visible before "see more," so it has to earn the click.
- Make it thought-provoking, blunt, a little polarising, funny, or weirdly specific. No fish without a hook.
- Techniques: start in the middle of the action, create a "gather round" feeling, open an information
  gap, or lead with the punchline.
- Aim it at one of these: their biggest fear (to ease it), biggest dream (to mirror it), biggest
  belief (to champion it), or biggest knowledge gap (to teach it).
- Keep it short. One line is often best. Never waste line 1 on setup.

Weak hook: "I want to share some thoughts on hiring for agencies."
Strong hook: "We once hired someone who downloaded all our client data and walked it to a competitor."

### The body
Use the story structure that's working on LinkedIn right now (see `references/personas-and-voice.md`
for the full storytelling breakdown):

**Normal → Complication → New Normal.**
- **Normal:** what life looked like before. Set the scene with a relatable character (Leon or a client).
- **Complication:** the problem, with real stakes. Highest tension. What was at risk?
- **New Normal:** what life looks like after, mirroring the opening, ending on the lesson or business point.

Leon is rarely the hero. The audience is. He's the guide who's been where they are.

Formatting:
- Short lines, generous white space, one thought per line. Easy to skim on a phone.
- Specific details (real numbers, real objects, real feelings) prove "this person gets me."
- Goldilocks emotion: genuine, not exaggerated, not flat.

### The close (CTA)
End with one easy question that a shy "LinkedIn lurker" could answer without admitting weakness.
Put a positive spin on it. If the question makes them think too hard or feel exposed, they won't comment.

Good: "What inspired you to start your agency? 👇"
Avoid: "What's the biggest mistake you're making right now?" (makes them admit failure publicly)

Where it fits the track, the next step can instead be: grab the Google Ads Guide, book a call,
or listen to a Boost episode. Don't force a pitch onto a personal-story post.

## Polls (a core Lead Gen format)

Polls pull silent viewers out of hiding so Leon can follow up in the DMs. Rules:
- 2-4 options. **Every option must be something Leon can easily follow up on** (each one opens a
  DM conversation with John or Corey).
- Add a short story or pain hook above the poll.
- When the question touches something they might be embarrassed to admit, reassure them the votes
  are anonymous (only the poster sees who voted).
- Always plan the follow-up: thank voters, then ask "what made you say that?" to open a real chat.

Example:
> Ever had that heart-stopping moment when a once-thriving ad campaign suddenly goes dark, right
> before a client check-in? 😬
>
> Be honest, what's the biggest growth hurdle in your agency right now?
> (Votes are anonymous, only I can see them.)
> - Doing too much myself
> - Keeping up with new trends
> - Scaling marketing
> - Measuring the right metrics
>
> Got another one? Drop it below 👇

The poll bank (15+ ready polls) is in `references/post-idea-bank.md`.

## Workflow

1. **Identify the track and the persona** (John or Corey). State them to yourself.
2. **Find the raw material.** If Leon gave a story or idea, use it. If not, pull from
   `references/post-idea-bank.md` (50+ ideas) or `references/story-bank.md` (his real stories,
   "cupcakes"). Prefer a real Leon story over a generic take every time.
3. **Pick the structure:** story post (Normal/Complication/New Normal), list/lesson post, or poll.
4. **Write the hook first.** Get it sharp before anything else.
5. **Draft the body** short and skimmable, one idea, real specifics.
6. **Add the easy CTA question** (or a soft next step that fits the track).
7. **Edit against Anti-Leon and the hard writing rules.** Strip em dashes, AI-isms, and any line
   that sounds cocky or preachy. Read it aloud in Leon's voice.

If you're missing a real detail that would make the post land (a number, the client's situation,
how it actually felt), ask Leon one quick question rather than inventing it. Never fabricate client
results, numbers, or stories.

## Output format

Default to giving the **ready-to-paste post** in a code block (so line breaks survive), then a
short note underneath. Like this:

```
[The post, formatted with the line breaks exactly as it should appear on LinkedIn]
```

**Track:** Expert / Lead Gen / Agency / Co-Founder
**For:** John or Corey
**Suggested visual:** (e.g. "a photo of you at CES" / "selfie from the event" / "no image, text only")
**Hook note:** one line on why the hook works, only if useful.

If Leon asks for a batch (e.g. "give me this week's posts"), give 3 posts across 3 different tracks,
each in its own code block with the same metadata.

For a docx deliverable, follow Leon's standing preference and use the docx skill, but default to
inline code blocks since posts get pasted straight into LinkedIn.

## Review / improve mode

When Leon pastes an existing draft and wants it reviewed, check against:
1. Does the hook earn the "see more" click, or is line 1 wasted on setup?
2. Is it written to one persona, or muddled between both?
3. One idea, or too many?
4. Any em dashes or AI-isms? (flag and remove)
5. Does it sound like Anti-Leon anywhere (cocky, preachy, hustle-y, flashy)?
6. Real specifics and a Leon story/voice, or generic?
7. Is there an easy CTA question?

Give: what works, what to change, then a rewritten version in a code block.

## Quick examples

**Co-Founder Track, for Corey, story post:**
```
I became a dad at 21.

At the time I was working nights at HEB, sleeping during the day, and writing for a tech blog in
whatever hours were left.

Everyone supported me. But I knew that wasn't the life I wanted to build.

Those night shifts taught me more than I realized. I'd listen to podcasts on the floor, learning
everything I could, watching other people try to claw their way out of the same rat race.

It took years. But that pressure is exactly why I push so hard for founders now.

If you started your business to build a different kind of life, I get it.

What pushed you to start yours? 👇
```
Track: Co-Founder. For: Corey. Visual: a photo of Leon from back then.

**Expert Track, for John, myth-debunk:**
```
Everyone tells you to hire the moment you're busy.

I think that's backwards.

You build the thing first, then you hire people to run it. Hire into chaos and you just pay someone
to be confused alongside you.

Before you make your next hire, ask yourself:
- What exactly will this person own?
- Does a documented process exist for it yet?
- Would I trust them to do it without me checking every day?

If the answer to any of those is no, you're not hiring too late. You're hiring too early.

When did you make your first real hire, and would you do it the same way again?
```
Track: Expert. For: John. Visual: text only, or a behind-the-scenes shot.
