## Long tail (200 to 400+ words): Joshua voice

**Job type:** Google Ads strategy and management, SaaS company, ongoing retainer

---

Howdy,

When you say your current campaigns are burning budget, is that a targeting problem or a conversion path problem? The fix is different for each.

I've looked at a lot of SaaS accounts where the click volume looks healthy but the CPA tells a different story. Usually it comes down to one of three things: keyword intent misalignment, landing page disconnect, or attribution gaps that make bad campaigns look okay. The audit tells you which.

Here's how I'd approach the first 30 days:

1. Full account audit: search terms, match types, quality scores, conversion setup.
2. Identify the top 3 leaks (almost always audience, creative, or attribution).
3. Restructure what needs it, pause what's hurting, and build a test to validate the hypothesis.
4. Weekly reporting cadence from day one so you always know what's moving and why.

I'm Google Ads certified, have managed over $2M in annual spend across SaaS and lead gen, and I run lean. No account manager in the middle. You talk to the person running the campaigns.

My rate for ongoing management is $X/month for accounts up to $Y in ad spend. Happy to share more specifics once I know your current monthly budget.

Any questions you may have?

Joshua

## Long tail (200 to 400+ words): Leon voice

**Job type:** SEO and content strategy for a B2B services firm, ongoing retainer

---

Howdy,

Are you starting from a thin content base, or do you have existing pages that are ranking but not converting?

The answer changes the strategy significantly. Starting thin means foundational technical cleanup and topical authority building first. Existing rankings that don't convert usually means a UX and intent alignment problem on the page itself.

Here's how I'd approach the first 30 days:

1. Technical audit: crawlability, indexation, Core Web Vitals, internal linking gaps.
2. Keyword and topical gap analysis vs. your three closest organic competitors.
3. Content plan with priority order based on traffic opportunity and conversion proximity.
4. Quick wins: find pages already on page two that can move with targeted optimization.

I've built organic programs for B2B service firms where we took a site from 400 to 12,000 monthly visitors in 14 months, primarily through technical fixes and a consistent content cadence. No tricks, no link schemes. Just clean structure and content Google can actually understand.

I use Rank Math for on-page, Ahrefs for research, and GA4 for attribution. You get a monthly report in plain English, not an agency dashboard full of numbers that don't connect to business outcomes.

Any questions you may have?

Leon

## Notes on these examples

- Every opener is a question tied to a specific diagnostic, not a generic "do you need help with X?"
- No em dashes appear anywhere.
- Both short examples hit under 50 words.
- Both long examples hit over 200 words.
- Neither example lands in the 100 to 149 word dead zone.
- The long examples include a plan section (numbered list), a credibility paragraph, and a logistics paragraph.
- All sign off with just the first name.
