# Cover Letter Playbook

## The data

This playbook is grounded in analysis of approximately 134,000 Upwork proposals.

**Reply rate by word count band:**

| Word count | Reply rate |
|---|---|
| Under 50 words | ~16% |
| 50 to 99 words | ~12% |
| 100 to 149 words | ~6.7% (worst) |
| 150 to 199 words | ~8% |
| 200 to 299 words | ~11% |
| 300 to 400+ words | ~14% |

The distribution is a U-curve. Both tails outperform the middle. The worst performing band is 100 to 149 words, which is also the most common length. Avoid it on purpose.

**Rule of thumb:** Pick a band before you write. If you land at 130 words, either cut to under 50 or expand past 200. Never polish a draft into the dead zone.

## Opener performance

The opener is the first thing the client reads. It determines whether they read the rest.

**What kills opens:**
- "I am a [title] with X years of experience..." (generic, skipped)
- Restating the job description back at the client (feels like a bot)
- Starting with "I" in general (signals self-focus)
- Praise openers: "What a great opportunity!" (signals desperation)

**What wins opens:**
- A question that proves you read the post. Specific detail from the posting, not a generic question.
- A short provocative claim followed immediately by evidence.
- Naming the client's problem in their terms, not your terms.

Example of a weak opener:
> "I am an experienced Google Ads specialist with over 7 years managing PPC campaigns for clients across multiple industries."

Example of a strong opener:
> "Is the conversion tracking issue you mentioned a GTM problem, or did Ads lose the tag on a recent site update?"

The strong version proves reading, shows expertise, and invites a reply. All in one sentence.

## Closer performance

Closers that invite a response outperform closers that declare intent.

**Highest performing closer in the dataset:**
> "Any questions you may have?"

This is counter-intuitive. It flips the dynamic. Instead of asking the client for something (a call, a reply, a decision), it offers them space. Use it when you want to test.

**Other closers that work:**
- "Worth a quick call?" (low friction, direct)
- "Want me to send a 10 minute Loom walking through how I'd approach this?" (adds value before commitment)

**Closers that underperform:**
- "I look forward to hearing from you." (passive, forgettable)
- "Please feel free to reach out." (sounds like a form letter)
- Long paragraphs about your availability or process. Save logistics for after they reply.

## Banned phrases

Do not use any of these, ever:

- "I want to absorb your vision"
- "delve into"
- "leverage"
- "unlock"
- "robust"
- "comprehensive"
- "seamlessly"
- "streamline"
- "actionable insights"
- "holistic approach"
- "game-changer"
- "cutting-edge"
- "in today's digital age"
- "I am passionate about"
- "results-driven"
- "I hit the ground running"

These phrases appear in a large share of losing proposals. Experienced clients have developed a filter for them.

## Format rules

- Salutation: `Howdy,` with the comma. No exceptions.
- No em dashes. Use commas, periods, or line breaks instead.
- Short to medium sentences. Fragments are fine.
- No bullet lists in short proposals. Bullets can work in long proposals for the plan section only.
- Sign off with the bidder's first name on its own line.
- Do not include a signature block, LinkedIn URL, or portfolio link in the letter body. Those go in the profile or attachments.

## Tone

Empathetic, not lecturing. The goal is to sound like someone who has seen this problem before and knows how to fix it, not like someone auditioning for a job.

The client has a problem. The cover letter is the beginning of a conversation, not a pitch deck. Write it that way.
