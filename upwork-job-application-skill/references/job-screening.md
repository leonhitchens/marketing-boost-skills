# Job Screening Checklist

Use this checklist in order. A single hard "no" disqualifies the job.

## 1. Service fit

Is the work something Ruskin Consulting actually delivers?

- Google Ads (Search, Display, Shopping, YouTube, Performance Max): yes
- SEO (on-page, technical, local, AI visibility): yes
- WordPress website builds (Elementor or Kadence only): yes
- Landing pages and conversion copy: yes
- GA4 and analytics setup: yes
- LinkedIn Ads: yes
- Meta Ads (Facebook / Instagram): yes
- Ecommerce PPC or SEO (budget $100+, no store-building): yes with caveats
- Store-building, product photography, fulfillment, logistics: no
- Pure copywriting or email marketing as primary: no
- Figma or UI/UX design: no
- Divi or Avada websites: no

If the job is ecommerce, confirm the work is PPC, SEO, or web, not operations.

## 2. Agency and consultation flags

- Post says "no agencies": skip unless the rate is unusually high (2x+ normal for scope) and the bidder can credibly present as a solo expert.
- Post says "consultation only" or "advice only": skip. These rarely convert to real work and attract scope creep.
- Post says "employee" or "full-time": skip. Ruskin Consulting does not take employee roles.

## 3. Client quality filter

Prefer clients who are:

- Payment verified
- Hire rate 30%+
- Based in: US, UK, Canada, Australia, New Zealand, Singapore, Portugal

Other geographies are not an automatic disqualification, but flag the rate risk. Clients in some markets will not pay rates that make the job worth taking.

## 4. Client spend and rating (counter-intuitive read)

This is important. Do not assume big spenders are better clients.

- **Whale clients ($500k+ lifetime spend, 4.8+ stars):** reply at roughly half the rate of mid-market. They are inundated with proposals and have high filtering standards. Worth a shot but do not over-invest in the proposal.
- **Mid-market sweet spot ($1k to $50k lifetime spend, 4.0 to 4.7 stars):** best reply rate. These clients have hired before, know what they want, and are not overwhelmed with top-tier proposals.
- **New clients (0 hires, unverified payment):** higher risk. Acceptable for small fixed-price jobs. Risky for ongoing retainers.

Note the client tier in the FIT verdict if you can read it from the posting.

## 5. Rate and budget fit

- Fixed-price jobs under $100: skip unless the scope is genuinely tiny (one audit, one landing page wireframe, one keyword list).
- Hourly jobs with a max rate below $35/hr: flag but do not auto-skip. Context matters.
- Jobs with no budget listed: proceed, but note the rate needs to be established early.

## 6. Scope and red flags

Watch for:

- Vague scope with a low budget: "help with all my marketing" for $500 total is a trap.
- Long lists of deliverables with no mention of timeline: scope creep risk. Flag in clarifying questions.
- "Looking for someone to be my right-hand person": usually means unpaid strategy time on top of paid execution.
- Multiple platforms expected for one flat rate: Google Ads + Meta Ads + SEO + content for $800/month is not viable.

## Output

After running the checklist, output:

```
FIT: yes / no / maybe
BIDDER: Joshua / Leon / either (ask)
WHY: one or two sentences
```

Then proceed to the cover letter if FIT is yes or maybe.
